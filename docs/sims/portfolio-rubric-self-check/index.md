## About This MicroSim

Chapter 25 grades the capstone portfolio with a 100-point rubric whose weights favor honest
analysis over impressive-looking numbers. This self-check turns the rubric into a chart you can
use while you work:

| Criterion | Points | Full credit means |
|---|---|---|
| Portfolio design | 15 | Objectives with Bloom levels, at least three types, sound type choices |
| Quality gate | 15 | Every new MicroSim at 70 or above and width-responsive |
| Instrumentation | 15 | Passing checks, one concept per statement, evidence classes justified |
| Usability test and peer review | 15 | Findings recorded, fixes made and re-scored |
| Data policy | 10 | Minimization and aggregate-only rules stated and enforced in the analysis |
| Portfolio fidelity report | 20 | Complete Chapter 19 structure, uncertainty shown, limits sentence first |
| Defense | 10 | Answers questions about measured versus designed claims |

Each criterion has a self-rating slider from 0 to 100 percent. Its bar shows the points earned at
that rating in blue and the points still available in grey, and the total appears above the chart.
Hover over a bar to read what full credit means. **Suggest next focus** highlights, in orange with
a thick outline, the criterion with the most points still available.

The suggestion is only a starting point. Points still available are one reason to work on a
criterion; another is dependency, since a fidelity report cannot be honest until the
instrumentation it rests on passes its checks. That is why the text box asks for your reason.

These ratings are your own self-assessment. They are not a grade, and the total is only as good as
your honesty about each "full credit means" statement.

**Learning objective:** The learner will assess a draft portfolio against the seven rubric
criteria and justify which criterion to improve first.

**Bloom's taxonomy level:** Evaluate (verb: *assess*)

## How to Use

1. Read the "full credit means" text for a criterion (hover over its bar, or use the table above).
2. Move that criterion's slider to the percentage of full credit your draft portfolio earns today.
   Repeat for all seven.
3. Read the total above the chart.
4. Press **Suggest next focus**. The criterion with the most points still available is
   highlighted.
5. In the text box, write why you will improve that criterion first, or why a different one
   should come first.

## Lesson Plan

### Audience

Teachers, instructional designers, learning-technology developers and learning-analytics
practitioners (college undergraduate and professional development).

### Duration

15-20 minutes

### Prerequisites

- The capstone brief, milestones and rubric in Chapter 25
- A draft portfolio, or the chapter's worked Ohm's-law portfolio plan as a stand-in

### Activities

1. **Rate with evidence (7 min):** For each criterion, write one piece of evidence from the draft
   (a validator score, a check-xapi.py report, a usability note) before moving its slider.
2. **Focus (3 min):** Press **Suggest next focus** and compare the suggestion with your own
   instinct.
3. **Justify (5 min):** In the text box, write a two-sentence reason for your first improvement
   that uses both points and dependency.
4. **Peer check (optional, 5 min):** Swap ratings with a partner. Each partner challenges one
   rating that seems too generous, citing the "full credit means" text.

### Assessment

- Every rating is backed by a concrete piece of evidence from the portfolio.
- The learner's reason names the points at stake and at least one dependency or risk.
- The learner can explain why the self-rated total is not a grade.
